\chapter{Timeline, Milestones and Risk Management}\label{ch:plan}

A research project rarely fails for technical reasons. It fails because a few
weeks disappear into a problem nobody noticed growing, or because the scope never
stopped expanding. This chapter is the defence: a schedule you can track, exit
criteria that tell you unambiguously when a phase is done, a weekly routine, and
a list of the risks most likely to hurt you, each with a plan.

\emph{evidence} for each one, not when you feel nearly done
(\cref{tab:plan:milestones}). At every milestone, tag the repository, for

\section{Weekly rhythm}\label{sec:plan:rhythm}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Monday (15 minutes).} Choose this week's two or three goals.
        Each must finish with something checkable, such as \enquote{the
        \code{recip\_unit} test passes}, not \enquote{work on the reciprocal}.
  \item \textbf{Every working session (5 minutes).} Start by rereading your last
        notebook entry, and end by writing a new one: what you did, what
        happened, and what you will do next. This is the habit that saves
        projects.
  \item \textbf{Friday (30 minutes).} Review the week against the plan. Update
        the Gantt chart and the risk register. Write the weekly status note
        (\cref{app:templates}).
  \item \textbf{Every two weeks.} Meet your supervisor with the status notes.
        Bring a decision you need help with, not just a progress report.
\end{itemize}

\begin{tipbox}[Protect deep-work blocks]
RTL debugging and verification need uninterrupted blocks of at least two to
three hours. Twelve hours in four solid blocks gets far more done than twelve
hours in twenty fragments.
\end{tipbox}

\section{Fitting the project to your credit load}\label{sec:plan:scope}

Under the ECTS system, one credit usually corresponds to roughly 25--30 hours of
work. Check the norms and rules for your course type at your
university~\cite{ch12-13:dtucoursebase,ch12-13:dtuprojectcourses}. The full plan
is about 26 weeks at 12--15 hours, roughly 310--390 hours. If you have less time,
cut whole phases rather than doing every phase badly
(\cref{tab:plan:scope}).

\section{Decision points}\label{sec:plan:decisions}

Some decisions should be made at fixed points, using evidence, so they do not
drift:

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item[End of week 4 --- frame configuration]
  Choose the sample rate, window and padding from the frame study. If no
  configuration under \qty{10}{\ms} meets the quality target, keep the best
  under \qty{16}{\ms} and report the trade-off honestly. That is still an answer
  to RQ2.
  \item[End of week 7 --- precision (M1)]
  If some block needs more than 24 bits to meet RQ1, give that block a small
  custom floating-point or logarithmic format, or relax the target and
  document it. Do not let Phase~1 run into week 9.
  \item[Week 12 --- FPGA readiness]
  If you have no working Vivado host by week 12 (\cref{ch:setup}), switch Phase~4
  to a \emph{simulation-only demo}: process recorded audio through the RTL and
  play the result back. Move the saved weeks into Phase~5.
  \item[Week 16 --- choose the track]
  Choose Track A or B based on your results so far, your interests and your
  supervisor's advice. Write the decision and its reasons in the notebook.
\end{description}

\section{When you get stuck}\label{sec:plan:stuck}

Getting stuck is normal. Staying stuck for a week is a process failure. Use this
protocol:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Timebox.} Give a problem two focused hours. Write down what you
        tried as you go.
  \item \textbf{Shrink it.} Build the smallest reproduction you can: one module,
        one test, one input. Half of all bugs are found while doing this.
  \item \textbf{Compare with the model.} In this project you almost always have a
        golden model. Find the \emph{first} signal or block where the two
        disagree.
  \item \textbf{Ask.} Ask your supervisor, or the tool's own community: the
        GitHub issues and discussions of Verilator and cocotb, the PYNQ support
        forum~\cite{ch12-13:pynqforum}, the Tiny Tapeout community for Track~B,
        or a general FPGA forum. Post the minimal reproduction, the exact tool
        versions, and what you already tried.
  \item \textbf{Route around it.} If the problem is not central to a research
        question, find a workaround, note it as a limitation, and move on.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{donebox}[You are using this chapter well if]
\begin{checklist}
  \item The Gantt chart and risk register are updated every Friday.
  \item Every milestone has a tag, a report and evidence for each exit criterion.
  \item Decisions at the four decision points are written down with reasons.
  \item No problem has stayed unsolved and unescalated for more than a week.
\end{checklist}
\end{donebox}
